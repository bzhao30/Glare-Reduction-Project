# Glare-Reduction-Project
When driving at night, headlights from other cars can often be too bright, causing discomfort or lack of vision for the driver. This poses a problem because headlights seem to be getting increasingly brighter, while there has not been new technology to actively reduce the glare on the windshield. For older drivers or those with sensitive eyes, the glare may become frightening or dangerous and jeopardizes people’s safety on the road. Therefore, the goal of this project is to create an effective way of actively reducing the glare on the windshield without compromising the driver’s vision.

The project has three main components: a Raspberry-Pi, Basys3 FPGA, and a LCD monitor. To find the location of the driver’s head and eyes, the Raspberry Pi takes advantage of the Open-CV library for face recognition. Taking advantage of Open-CV, the coordinates of the driver are sent to the FPGA. To find the location of the incoming headlights, the FPGA is connected to a stereo camera setup which reads in video and determines the three-dimensional coordinates of the incoming headlights. Then, the FPGA calculates the location on the LCD panel to darken. The windshield is simulated by the LCD panel, since liquid crystal is transparent and can be made opaque by adding electric field via Indium Tin Oxide electrodes attached to the existing LCD TV circuitry.
